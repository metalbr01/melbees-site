Minesweeper online with hints from Hug

Play classic Minesweeper in your browser, free and with nothing to install. It is a logic game: every number counts the neighboring mines, and Hug, our teacher, gives an explained hint whenever you get stuck.

This game needs JavaScript. Turn it on to play.

How to play

The board is a grid of closed cells. Some of them hide mines, and your job is to open every cell that has no mine. Tap or click a cell to open it. If it hides a mine, the game ends. If not, it shows a number: how many mines sit in the eight cells around it. An empty cell, with no number, has no mine anywhere near, so the game opens all of its neighbors for you, in a cascade.

When you work out that a cell holds a mine, mark it with a flag. On a computer, use the right mouse button. On a phone, hold your finger on the cell (a long press) or turn on the "Flag mode" button and tap as usual. A flag only helps you remember: it does not open or protect anything. The counter shows how many mines are left to mark, and the timer starts at your first click. With a keyboard, use the arrow keys to move, Space to open and F to flag.

There are three levels: Beginner (9 by 9 with 10 mines), Intermediate (16 by 16 with 40) and Advanced (30 by 16 with 99). Every board grows from a seed. If you open the page with ?seed=123 in the address, you get the same board every time for the same first click, so you can send the link to a friend and compare who solves it faster.

Basic strategy

The first click is always safe. The game only places the mines after it, and leaves out the cell you opened and the eight around it, so your first move almost always opens an area with numbers. From there on, nothing is luck as long as there is a cell that logic can prove.

Start with the numbers that are easiest to read. If a number has exactly as many closed cells around it as its value, all of them are mines. If a number already has all of its mines marked, the other closed cells around it are safe, and you can open them. These two ideas solve most of the Beginner level.

Then look at two neighboring numbers. This is the subset rule: if the closed cells of one number all sit inside the closed cells of another, compare what each one still needs. In the 1-1 pattern, both numbers need one mine and share the same cells, except that the second has one extra cell: that extra cell is safe. In the 1-2 pattern, the second needs one more mine than the first and has exactly one more cell: that cell is a mine.

Sometimes no simple rule works. A good player then tests the possibilities: imagine where the mines could be and see whether some cell is safe, or a mine, in all of them. If nothing is certain even then, what is left is a guess: opening a cell without proof. A guess is not logic, it is calculated risk. Pick the cell with the lowest chance of a mine, ideally far from where you want to make progress, and accept that sometimes it fails.

Hug's hints

The "Hug's hint" button makes Hug look at the board like a teacher: first he looks for a simple counting rule, then the subset rule and, last, he checks every possible way to place the mines along the border. He only highlights a cell when it is safe, or a mine, in all of those ways, and he explains why in plain words. If nothing is certain, he says so and shows the lowest-risk cell, warning you that it is a guess. Use the hint to learn the reasoning, not only to move forward.

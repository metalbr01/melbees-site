Will the Ocarina speedrun tricks survive the remake?

The Ocarina of Time remake is out on Switch 2, and Link can jump and dash now. Do the 1998 original's speedrun tricks survive? Here's what we can already say.

By Melbees Dev · Published on November 22, 2026 · Updated on November 28, 2026 · 4 min read

Wrong warp in the Ocarina remake? Whoever tests it first, tell me.

Pow

My favorite Zelda is still A Link to the Past, on the Super Nintendo. I'm a nostalgic 2D Zelda guy: I like Link's Awakening on Game Boy, and Echoes of Wisdom, which brings back that top-down view from Link to the Past. Ocarina is also on my list of games I love most, so when the remake hit Switch 2 on November 5, 2026, I ended up with a slightly nerdy question.

Do the speedrun tricks from the 1998 Ocarina still work in the remake? Or does the new game bring movement and rules that push the times down even further? Because Link can jump and dash now, something the original never had in the same way. And more freedom of movement usually means new shortcuts for speedrunners.

How the original Ocarina Any% gets run

If you've never watched an Ocarina speedrun, it's kind of hypnotic. The Any% record (beat the game as fast as possible, no restrictions) is 3:47.9, set on July 13, 2024. Under four minutes, start to credits.

How is that even possible? Glitch (a game bug used on purpose) stacked on glitch, each one heavier. In order:

  • 2012: the wrong warp shows up, a portal that sends you somewhere the game never planned. The most popular route for 12 years went through a door in the Deku Tree and landed at Ganon's tower during the castle collapse.
  • January 2020: a memory trick called Stale Reference Manipulation lets runners warp straight to the credits. The record drops from 16:58 to 12:59 within days.
  • 2024: a nearly accidental find with corrupted polygons opens a new route from the second dungeon and cuts about a minute off the record at the time.

speedrun.com also splits Ocarina into categories that limit different techniques: No Wrong Warp, Defeat Ganon and Glitchless don't restrict the same things, and some are split further by whether SRM, the memory manipulation, is allowed. It's not one big "banned" block.

What the remake changes

Nintendo promises completely overhauled visuals, orchestral music, voice acting, expanded cutscenes and new dialogue. On gameplay: jumping and dashing (Link can jump and dash now), enhanced controls and a modern camera, plus aiming with buttons or motion. You can play the ocarina with buttons or through the microphone, by humming or with instruments. And there's new lore, like a Hall of Fallen Foes.

What Nintendo does not mention: difficulty, length, or whether the original's bugs are still in there. A reliable test of the old tricks in the remake hasn't shown up yet.

The provocation: are the bugs back?

In the original, the bugs pushed the Any% down to absurd times: routes that skip almost the whole game and go straight to the ending, the credits. So here's the big question: will the remake have the same bugs, and with them the same routes that cut everything? Or did the new game close those doors, or open different ones? If you know, that's the answer I most want to hear.

The remake's board, 17 days in

On November 22, 17 days after launch, the game's speedrun.com page (created on June 9, 2026, well before release) looked like this:

  • One category, provisional, called "* PENDING *".
  • 68 submitted runs, from November 5 to 22, by 45 different people.
  • 58 awaiting review, 10 rejected and none verified.

Those times don't mean anything yet. With only the provisional category, nobody has declared what they were going for. Among the 58 under review, the shortest is 12 seconds and the longest 8h30, with a median of 12,013 s (3h20min13s). Don't stack that against the original's record: there's no category yet to back a "record" in the remake.

So why zero verified? It's on purpose, and the moderators explain it in a post on speedrun.com itself: only one category, "Pending", opened at launch, to catch any run submitted before the boards fully open. Those runs wait in the queue, unverified, while the community hashes out rules and categories. Once the boards are set, the moderators will recategorize and verify the runs that fit the rules.

For a yardstick, two recent Zeldas. For Tears of the Kingdom, the first submitted run was done 6 days after launch, on May 18, 2023, and verified the next day. For Echoes of Wisdom, the first verified submission came in on October 10, 2024, 14 days after launch. The remake is at 17 days and zero, but there the moderators are holding submissions on purpose, so the comparison doesn't hold. For another snapshot of a brand-new page, GTA VI was at day three with no categories and no runs.

If somebody tests the wrong warp in the remake first, tell me. And if the new game has shortcuts of its own, I want to know which one you found first.

GTA VI memes: sick day, internship, 30 fps and the heist at checkout

The GTA VI memes that became Melbees videos: calling in sick, an internship included, 30 fps, checkout, single-player and more. What each joke means.

By Melbees · Published on November 22, 2026 · Updated on November 22, 2026 · 3 min read

A good joke needs no victim.

Red

When GTA VI came out, the internet did what it does best: it turned everything into a joke. Skipping work to play, chores inside the game that look like real life, frame rates, the price, the partner who vanished into the screen. We did our part and turned each of those topics into a short video with Mel and Pow. This guide explains each joke, where it comes from and what our game has to do with it.

The video that went out on social media. It only loads when you tap play.

"Called in sick"

It is the launch-day meme: people planning to "get sick" to play. Our version became the video "Called in sick". The charm is that everyone knows the urge, even without using the excuse. We do not encourage skipping work: the video is about the wish, and the wish is universal.

"Bought GTA VI, got an internship"

After launch, some players said a good part of the early game is made of everyday chores, like washing dishes, buying flowers and renovating the house. The playful nickname that spread was "Grand Theft Errands". Our piece became a Mel karaoke about the "shift". The song's conclusion: in Melbees the shift lasts 5 minutes, and your brain is the boss.

"30 fps, 1 fps, 4K"

Frame rates entered the debate right at the start: according to Digital Foundry, the consoles aim for 30 fps with no 60 fps mode, and that became a talking point among fans. Our joke was on ourselves: Melbees runs at "1 fps" in Mel's stop-motion dance, and your brain does the rest of the rendering, in "4K". It is a joke about every game having its own kind of quirk.

"GTA VI's first heist was at checkout"

Price became a topic too: the Standard edition was announced in Brazil at R$ 449.90, according to Tecnoblog and MeuPS. Mel, with an empty wallet, sang a version of a traditional Mexican folk song, and the Daily came in at "R$ 0.00. Every day". The joke is never about people who bought the game, only about Mel's own wallet.

"Single-player" and "My big chase"

The meme about the partner who vanished into the game (the "GTA widows") gave us "Single-player": Mel at a table for two, and her answer is to call the other person back to multiplayer, in the Melbees Duel. As for "Wanted" and "My big chase", they swap wanted-level stars for hexes: "Wanted: one five-letter word". Here the lead is the Pollen number.

"Cheat code"

Someone types a code on the phone and nothing happens. The closing line is our signature: "There is no cheat code for this. Just reasoning."

What all the jokes share

  • The target is always Mel or Pow, never the people who play.
  • Each one ends with the same call: the 5-minute Daily Challenge.
  • We use no music, logo or font from anyone else, and we have no connection to Rockstar.
Question: why do we avoid making fun of people who play?

Because the charm of a meme is the crowd feeling. A joke that picks a culprit becomes an argument, and arguments scare off people who only wanted to laugh.

Any%, 100% and glitchless: why one game has several world records

Hollow Knight has a 4:29 record and a 26:38 record, both world records. Here's what Any%, 100% and glitchless mean, with real speedrun.com times.

By Melbees · Published on November 22, 2026 · Updated on November 22, 2026 · 2 min read

Same game, different rules, different records.

Pow

Hollow Knight has a world record of 4:29.34 and another of 26:38. Both sit on the same speedrun.com board, and neither one is wrong. What changes is the category: each one is a different rule for "finishing the game", and each has its own record.

The names you'll see on every board

  • Any% ("any percent"): finish as fast as the rules allow, no matter how much of the game you collect along the way. It's generally one of the shortest.
  • 100%: finish while doing everything the game counts as "complete". What goes into that "everything" changes from game to game.
  • Glitchless: finish without the glitches the community decided to ban. The list of banned glitches is per game, too.

There are plenty of others, and the name is just a summary. The category description on the board is what rules.

Why there isn't just one record

Because you can only compare times from runs that follow the same rules. On a single board, the record would always belong to the wildest shortcut, and nobody running without glitches would ever show up. Splitting by category keeps each race fair.

Same games, very different times

On November 22, 2026, speedrun.com showed these records:

  • Hollow Knight: Any%, 4:29.34; All Skills (collecting every ability), 26:38. The second takes almost 6 times as long as the first.
  • Portal: Out of Bounds (using glitches to leave the map), 5:00.735; Inbounds (staying inside the map), 7:13.425; Glitchless, 14:23.43. Glitchless takes 2.9 times as long as Out of Bounds.
  • Celeste: Any%, 24:44.083; 100%, 1:37:16.372. The 100% run takes 3.9 times as long as Any%.
  • Super Mario 64: here the categories go by how many stars you collect. 0 Star, 6:14.19; 120 Star, 1:35:14, which is 15 times longer.

And every one of those times is a "world record", each in its own category.

One more thing that changes the number you see: each board picks which stopwatch counts. The Hollow Knight Any% record, for example, is 4:29.34 without loading screens, but 6:38 in plain real time.

Seven minutes, 47 seconds. That's the Any% record for GTA Vice City on speedrun.com, and yes, it's real (the catch is coming up). GTA VI came out on November 19 and it's all anyone talks about, so we went to see how the older GTAs are doing over there, using the site's public API.

First, what's a "category"?

A speedrun is beating a game as fast as you can, and every way of beating it gets its own ranking. Any% allows whatever the rules allow. 100% wants everything the game tracks. There are niche ones too, like "all hidden packages". The long version lives in our article on Any%, 100% and glitchless.

Five games, five boards

We checked GTA III, Vice City, San Andreas, IV and V. All five have a page on the site, created between November 7 and 16, 2014. Counting full-game categories, III has 7, Vice City 8, IV 6, and San Andreas and V tie at 12. Below, the speedrunners on each game's main board (when a version has a restriction in its name, like "No SSU", we took the one with more people):

Of the five boards we compared, Vice City has the most speedrunners: 514. Keep in mind each board has its own rules, so this says who has the crowd, not which game is "best". Its oldest run is dated April 7, 2008, more than six years before the page existed on the site. As for pace, the board shows 65 runs dated 2026 and 46 dated 2025. One warning: it keeps only each person's best run, so those are the dates of today's best runs, not a headcount of who ran in each year.

Game by game

  • GTA III (2001): the Any% (No ASE) record is 59min16s, set November 14, 2026, five days before GTA VI launched. The 100% is 4h02min12s, with 47 speedrunners.
  • Vice City (2002): Any% (No SSU) sits at 47min54s and the unrestricted Any% at 7min47s, so the longer time is 6.15 times the shorter one. Only 58 people have a time on the unrestricted board, against 514 on the other. The 100% is 4h19min40s.
  • San Andreas (2004): 12 categories, from Any% and all story missions to tags, snapshots, horseshoes, oysters and "69/70 unique stunts". Any% (No AJS) is 2h49min05s, set November 13, six days before GTA VI launched. The 100% takes 10h27min06s, with 35 speedrunners.
  • GTA IV (2008): 3h19min51s for Any%, set February 26, 2023, with 78 speedrunners. The 100% (7h31min38s) has just 9.
  • GTA V (2013): Any% is 2h11min29s, set March 9, 2025, with 248 speedrunners and 357 runs on the board. Director Mode and Golf are in the category list too.

The catch behind 7:47

If you saw "7:47" and figured someone clears Vice City during a coffee break, hang on. That time is from the board without "No SSU" in the name, and the other one (47min54s) bans SSU, short for Script Stack Underflow. Each of those tags is a ban on that board: in GTA III, "No ASE" means no Arbitrary Script Execution; in San Andreas, "No AJS" bans ASE plus Arbitrary Jump in Script. The full rules sit on each board's page. Same pattern elsewhere: San Andreas has 32min03s on the unrestricted Any% and 2h49min05s on "No AJS"; GTA III has 35min57s on the unrestricted one and 59min16s on "No ASE". Before you compare two times, read the board's name.

Is anyone still running these?

Yes, on very different boards. In 2026, the GTA V Any% has 43 runs dated this year (29 from 2025), and San Andreas (No AJS) has 24 (12 from 2025). At the quiet end: the GTA IV 100% has 9 speedrunners, with runs from 2016 to 2026 and only one from 2026, and the unrestricted San Andreas Any%, 21 speedrunners, has every run dated 2026.

And GTA VI? It has a page on the site, but that's a different story, in the next article.

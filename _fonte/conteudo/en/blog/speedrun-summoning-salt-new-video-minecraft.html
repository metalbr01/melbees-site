Summoning Salt is known for making few videos and making them very well, all about the history of speedrunning records. The newest one, published on November 14, 2026, is called Minecraft Speedrunning: A Brief History. On the Melbees blog we do not retell what the video shows. We stick to what can be confirmed on its page and add numbers of our own, taken from a speedrun.com query.

That is everything we claim about the content. You find out the rest by watching, and the description doubles as a reading list for anyone who likes following credits.

Is an hour and thirteen long for this channel?

We counted the 30 videos on the channel's public videos page. The average length is 61.8 minutes, and 12 of them run past an hour. The Minecraft video, at 73.7 minutes, is the ninth longest of the 30, about 19% above the average. So it is a big video, but well within the house style. If you have never watched one, you could start with a shorter one, such as the 28-minute video about level 5-2 of Super Mario Bros., and come back.

How Minecraft looks on speedrun.com today

To add context the video does not need to give, we looked at the Minecraft: Java Edition leaderboard on speedrun.com. For each full-game category, the query returns the current best time:

  • Any%: 31.852 seconds, set on December 22, 2025.
  • All Advancements: 13 minutes and 46.37 seconds, set on September 5, 2026.

Two readings only arithmetic allows. First: the documentary runs 139 times longer than the Any% record. Second: the video is 5.4 times longer than the All Advancements record. Telling the history of how we got to such short times takes longer than doing the thing itself.

One caution: the API returned one record per category, without splitting subcategories (such as the kind of world used). Read these as the best listed time in each category on the query date, not as the rule for any specific type of run. We do not name the runners here; each run's link is in our query data.

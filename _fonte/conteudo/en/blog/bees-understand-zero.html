For a child, understanding that "nothing" is also a number takes years. For human history it took centuries: many civilizations counted and calculated for a long time without a symbol for the empty set. So the question sounds almost absurd: could a bee, with a brain the size of a seed, understand zero?

Researchers in Australia and France asked exactly that, and the result came out in 2018 in the journal Science. In this article you will see how the test worked, what the bees did, what the study lets us say and what it leaves out.

The test: pick the card with fewer

The experiment used honey bees, the species that makes the honey you buy. The scientists trained the bees to choose, between two cards, the one with fewer items, and they got sugar when they were right. One card might have two dots, the other four. After many rounds the bees learned the rule.

The decisive step came later. In a test, one card was completely blank and the other had dots. With no training on blank cards at all, the bees chose the blank one, as if they knew that "nothing" is less than "something". They were even more reliable when the gap was large: a blank card against many dots was easier than a blank card against a single dot, the same pattern we see when people compare ordinary numbers.

What this shows

The result means the bees place zero on the number line: it comes before one, as the smallest value. Science News pointed out that they were the first invertebrates to pass a test of this kind, and the French research agency CNRS summed it up by saying bees treat zero as lower than any whole number. Before them, only a few vertebrates, such as certain primates and an African grey parrot, had passed similar tests.

It is a curious fact about the evolution of thinking: the ability to grasp "none" may have appeared more than once, in very different animals, instead of being a feature of big brains alone. A honey bee brain has about 1 million neurons, while ours has about 86 billion, roughly 86 thousand times more.

What the study does not say

It is worth staying careful. The study does not prove that a bee can do arithmetic with zero, such as adding or multiplying. According to Science News, using zero in calculations is still something only humans do. What the bees did was treat emptiness as a smaller quantity. That is a lot for an insect, but it is not all of mathematics. It was also a lab experiment with careful training, not something bees do on their own in the garden.

True or false?

Test what you learned before opening each answer.

The bees in the study were trained on blank cards before the test.

False. The training was only about choosing the card with fewer items. The blank card showed up only in the test, and they picked it anyway.

The study showed that bees add and subtract with zero.

False. It showed that they treat zero as less than one. Doing calculations with zero is a different skill, which the research did not test.

Bees were the first invertebrates to pass this kind of test.

True. Science News described them that way, after some vertebrates such as certain primates and a parrot had passed.

Zero is on the Melbees honeycomb too

In Melbees, zero is an old friend of every player. Reading "nothing" is part of the reasoning. Lil, one of the worker bees in the game, would buzz with approval.

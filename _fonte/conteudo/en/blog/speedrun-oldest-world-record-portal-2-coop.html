Portal 2 became one of my favorites playing it with my daughter. It's another game I've played a lot with her, and it's the kind you play together, not alone.

Local co-op, explained like a human

Portal 2 is Valve's game, from 2011. The whole trick is a gun that opens two portals: step into one, come out the other. Sounds easy, until the first room where the floor disappears and the only way out is a wall way up high.

In co-op you each control a robot, Atlas or P-Body, and you each get your own portal gun. That's four portals in one room, and the puzzle only clicks if both of you are thinking at once. Locally the screen is split: your half, their half, one robot each, same couch.

Laughing, arguing, getting mad, and loving it anyway

It's great fun in co-op, especially local, sharing the couch. You laugh, you have to work together, you argue, you get mad, but in the end it's super cool. And in my opinion, anybody picks up the idea fast.

She had a blast, and the best part wasn't even clearing the room. It was the debate: one of us poking holes in the other's idea, then swapping, testing until we found the one that worked.

The game teaches without lecturing. Nobody reads a manual: the room hands you a problem, you try, you fail, you try another way.

Creativity and logic

Portal 2 pushes creativity and originality, which is what we try to build into our games. Plenty of rooms have more than one answer: the one the designers had in mind, the one you made up, and sometimes both work.

Our main game, the only one out so far, is The Secret of Bees, and it also asks for creativity, logical reasoning, general knowledge and problem solving. There's a part where you think before you act. If you want to try it, the Daily Challenge has one a day, right in your browser. No portals, no robots, no split screen, but the same "think before you jump".

So why a speedrun detour?

Because Portal 2 has a record that's been sitting on top of its board since 2019. Cooperative Game: 7:48.2, set on February 16, 2019. Up to November 22, 2026, that's 7 years, 9 months and 6 days.

But 7 and a half minutes for the whole co-op campaign? Way too short. So I opened the run.

On the run page, the comment says: "This literally took us 5 attempts and I've never ran course 6 lol". The demo file is named ch6_ss_wr.zip. And the community rules split co-op into three subcategories: All Main Courses (courses 1 to 5), Any% (to the end of course 5) and All Courses, the only one that includes course 6.

Everything points to a course 6 run only. Since it has no subcategory set, it sits at the top of the board whenever nobody filters anything.

The full-campaign records

One subcategory at a time, the board looks like this:

  • All Main Courses: 25:13.333, set on October 23, 2026.
  • Any%: 17:42.567, set on December 12, 2021.
  • All Courses: 33:29.2, set on October 23, 2026.

The oldest of the three, Any%, is 1,806 days old, almost 5 years.

One thing that trips people up: in Portal 2, most runs aren't timed by the wall clock, they're timed by demo time, from the file the game records.

Moral of the story

Before you call a record "the oldest", open the run. That 7:48.2 is still there, no subcategory set, on top of the unfiltered board since February 16, 2019.

Portal 2 became one of my favorites playing it with my daughter. It's another game I've played a lot with her, and it's the kind you play together, not alone.

Local co-op, explained like a human

Portal 2 is Valve's game, from 2011. The basic trick is a gun that opens two portals: you step into one, you come out the other. Sounds easy, until you hit the first room where the floor disappears and the only way out is a wall way up high.

In co-op you each control one of two robots, Atlas and P-Body, and each of you has your own portal gun. That's four portals in one room, and the puzzle only works if both of you are thinking at once. Locally the screen is split: your half, their half, one robot each, same couch.

Laughing, arguing, getting mad, and loving it anyway

It's great fun in co-op, especially local, sharing the couch. You laugh, you have to work together, you argue, you get mad, but in the end it's super cool. And in my opinion, anybody picks up the idea fast.

The game teaches without lecturing. Nobody reads a manual: the room hands you a problem, you try, you fail, you try another way.

Creativity and logic

Portal 2 pushes creativity and originality, which is what we try to build into our games. Plenty of rooms have more than one answer: the one the designers had in mind, the one you made up, and sometimes both work.

Our main game, the only one out so far, is The Secret of Bees, and it also asks for creativity, logical reasoning, general knowledge and problem solving. There's a part where you think before you act. If you want to try it, the Daily Challenge has one a day, right in your browser. The Secret isn't Portal 2, of course: no portals, no robots, no split screen.

So why a speedrun detour?

Because I was looking at old speedrun records: the main categories of 10 games, through the speedrun.com API, hunting for the oldest one still standing. The top of the list was Portal 2, Cooperative Game: 7:48.2, set on February 16, 2019. From that date to November 22, 2026 is 7 years, 9 months and 6 days.

But 7 and a half minutes for the whole co-op campaign looked too short to me. So I opened the run.

On the run page, the comment says: "This literally took us 5 attempts and I've never ran course 6 lol". The demo file is named ch6_ss_wr.zip. The community rules (rules.portal2.sr) split co-op into three subcategories: All Main Courses (courses 1 to 5), Any% (to the end of course 5) and All Courses, the only one that includes course 6. That suggests a course 6 run only, but it doesn't prove it. The run also has no subcategory set, which is why it sits at the top of the board when nobody filters anything.

The full-campaign records

Filtering one subcategory at a time:

  • All Main Courses: 25:13.333, set on October 23, 2026.
  • Any%: 17:42.567, set on December 12, 2021.
  • All Courses: 33:29.2, set on October 23, 2026.

The oldest of the three, Any%, is 1,806 days old, almost 5 years.

One stopwatch note: the API tags these times as real time, but the community rules (rules.portal2.sr) say most Portal 2 runs use demo time.

So what's the oldest record?

With what I have, I can't say. I only checked the Portal 2 run. The other nine games weren't checked subcategory by subcategory, and a 2020 or 2021 record could have the same catch, or not.

That 7:48.2 is still sitting there, with no subcategory set, at the top of the unfiltered board since February 16, 2019.

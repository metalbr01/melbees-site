With GTA VI on everyone's lips, the most common conversation online is about time: how many hours the campaign has, how much of the week it will eat, who has "finished" it already. We made two jokes about that. In one, Mel claims she finished GTA VI in 67 hours and the Daily in 5 minutes. In the other, the math is direct: 100 hours against 5 minutes a day. The humor works because the question is real. How do you fit a giant game and a tiny habit into the same life?

Two sizes of game, two ways to play

Long games and short games ask different things from you.

  • Long game: it wants a block of time, steady attention and a will to dive in. It shines on weekends or at night, when you are not in a hurry.
  • Short daily game: it wants a pause of a few minutes, every day. It fits in a queue, over coffee, on the bus or right before you open the big game.

They do not get in each other's way. The long game is the meal, and the daily one is the habit of drinking water: a little at a time, every day, and you notice the difference after a few weeks.

The 100-hour math

Let us do the numbers without drama. If you play two hours a day, 100 hours take fifty days. If you play only on weekends, four hours a week, that is 25 weeks. Five minutes a day over fifty days adds up to a little over four hours. So the Daily Challenge uses roughly 4% of the time a 100-hour game asks for, and still leaves you with a reasoning routine.

Question: how many days of the Daily Challenge (5 minutes each) fit into 100 hours?

100 hours are 6,000 minutes. Divided by 5, that is 1,200 days, more than three years of one challenge a day.

Building the routine in 4 steps

  1. Pick a trigger. A small habit sticks more easily when it is attached to another one: after coffee, when you sit on the bus, before you open the big game.
  2. Agree on the time. Five minutes is the ceiling for the Daily Challenge. If a round runs longer, that is fine, but it does not need to.
  3. Think only about today's. The challenge is the one of the day, and the conversation is about the habit, not about catching up on what you missed.
  4. Save the big game for big time. That way neither one turns into a chore.

Why one challenge a day appeals

There is something good about a game that ends. The Melbees Daily Challenge hides secret words in a honeycomb of hexes: Hangman crossed with Minesweeper. You choose between Match and Discard for each letter, read the Pollen, which shows how many right letters are on the neighboring hexes, and slowly close the honeycomb. When it is done, it is done. Tomorrow there is another one, a small promise that makes you want to come back.

That is also why we made the "cheat code" joke: in action series, a typed code solves everything. Here there is no code. Only reasoning, and it gets better with use.

An honest talk about games and time

No game is "better" for being bigger or "worse" for being smaller. There are games that fit your week and games that do not. GTA VI is the topic of the moment and, for many people, worth the 100 hours. For people with busy days, the small game is a way to keep playing.

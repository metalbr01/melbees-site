GTA VI launched on November 19, and speedrun.com, the site where people log speedrun records, already has a page for it. Runs? Zero. On day three, November 22, 2026, the board was empty. Relax, that's normal: none of the big launches below had a verified run by day three either.

What the page had on day three

  • Page created on November 9, 2026, ten days before launch.
  • Categories: zero. No Any%, no 100%.
  • Submitted runs: zero, not even ones waiting for review.
  • Game rules on the site: real time only, video and verification required, no milliseconds. The page also allows emulators.
  • Listed platforms: PlayStation 5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S.
  • Moderation: 3 moderators on the list.

How long the other giants took

Simple math: days from launch to the submission of the first verified run, meaning one a moderator approved. In brackets, the day the oldest verified run was actually played.

The first verified run landed 6 to 28 days after launch, with a median of 15. By the date the runs were played, it's 1 to 25 days. And every one of those games had its page up before release, 8 to 31 days early.

GTA VI is following the script: page created 10 days ahead, and on day three, no categories. Day three is way too early to expect runs.

Console vs PC, and the fight that comes later

The game's listing already has a platform variable with Console and PC options. But with no categories, there's no board for it yet. Moderators are the ones who create categories and rules. The older GTAs, on the other hand, already have packed boards.

Can we tell if GTA VI will get an Any% category?

Not yet. On November 22 the page had no categories at all, and moderators are the ones who create them and set the rules. That can change overnight.

What gets me, and I've never even played it

I've never played GTA VI. I'm more into co-op and logic games. But you have to admire the art that went into it, and what I admire even more is the wrapper. The mundane chores people flat out refuse to do in real life, like housework, become the fun part once they're inside a game. Change the context, change the packaging, and your brain starts enjoying what it used to dodge. Going from real life to a virtual one changes how you see the exact same task, and that says a lot about how our heads work.

My favorite Zelda is still A Link to the Past, on the Super Nintendo. I'm a nostalgic 2D Zelda guy: I like Link's Awakening on Game Boy, and Echoes of Wisdom, which brings back that top-down view from Link to the Past. Ocarina is also on my list of games I love most, so when the remake hit Switch 2 on November 5, 2026, I ended up with a slightly nerdy question.

I wanted to know if the speedrun tricks from the 1998 Ocarina still work in the remake, or if the new game brings dynamics and rules that push the times down even further. It probably has more content than the original, but will speedrunners finish faster anyway?

How the original Ocarina Any% gets run

If you've never watched an Ocarina speedrun, it's kind of hypnotic. The Any% record (finish the game as fast as possible, no restrictions) is 3:47.9, set on July 13, 2024, according to speedrun.com. Under four minutes, start to credits.

How is that even possible? It came from stacking heavier and heavier glitches (game bugs used on purpose). In order:

  • 2012: the wrong warp shows up, a portal that sends you somewhere the game didn't plan for. GamesRadar reports that the most popular route for 12 years went through a door in the Deku Tree and ended up at Ganon's tower during the castle collapse.
  • January 2020: Kotaku explained that a memory trick called Stale Reference Manipulation allowed a warp straight to the credits. The record fell from 16:58 to 12:59 within days.
  • 2024: per GamesRadar, a nearly accidental find with corrupted polygons created a new route from the second dungeon, which cut about a minute off the record at the time.

speedrun.com also splits Ocarina into categories that limit different techniques: No Wrong Warp, Defeat Ganon and Glitchless don't restrict the same things, and some of them are split further by whether SRM, the memory manipulation, is allowed. So it's not one big "banned" block.

What the remake changes, per Nintendo

The official Nintendo page talks about completely overhauled visuals, orchestral music, voice acting, expanded cutscenes and new dialogue. On gameplay it mentions jumping and dashing (Link can jump and dash now), enhanced controls and a modern camera, plus aiming with buttons or motion. You can play the ocarina with buttons or through the microphone, by humming or with instruments. There's some new lore too, like a Hall of Fallen Foes.

What the page does not say: anything about difficulty, length, or whether the original's bugs are still there. I looked in more places, and I haven't found a reliable test of those tricks in the remake yet.

The provocation: are the bugs back?

In the original, the bugs pushed the Any% down to absurdly low times: routes that skip almost the whole game and go straight to the ending, the credits. So here's the big question: will the remake have the same bugs, and with them those same routes that cut everything? Or did the new game close those doors, or open different ones? I don't know, and so far I haven't found a reliable test. If you do know, that's the answer I most want to hear.

The original never had jump and dash in the same way, and more freedom of movement usually opens shortcuts for speedrunners. That the remake has more content than the original is just my impression; the official page doesn't say so.

What speedrun.com shows today

I checked the game's page today, November 22, 17 days after launch. It was created on June 9, 2026, well before the game came out. The snapshot:

  • One category, provisional, called "* PENDING *".
  • 68 submitted runs, from November 5 to 22, by 45 different people.
  • 58 awaiting review, 10 rejected and none verified.

The submitted times don't tell us much yet. With only the provisional category, nobody has declared what they were chasing. Among the 58 under review, the shortest time is 12 seconds and the longest 8h30, with a median of 12,013 s (3h20min13s). Don't compare that with the original's record: there's no category yet to back a "record" in the remake.

Why zero verified? The moderators explain it in a post on speedrun.com itself: on purpose, only one category, "Pending", was open at launch, to catch any run submitted before the boards fully open. Those runs aren't verified for now and sit in the queue while the community discusses rules and categories. Once the boards are final, the moderators say they'll recategorize and verify the runs that fit the rules.

For a yardstick, I looked at two recent Zeldas. For Tears of the Kingdom, the first submitted run I found was done 6 days after launch, on May 18, 2023, and verified the next day. For Echoes of Wisdom, the first verified submission came in on October 10, 2024, 14 days after launch. The remake is at 17 days and still has zero, but there the moderators are holding submissions on purpose, so the yardstick isn't fair. For another snapshot of a brand-new page, GTA VI is at day three with no categories and no runs.
